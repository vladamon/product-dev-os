# Talking to Humans — Giff Constable

**Weight: STRONG — lightweight practical extraction**

*Talking to Humans* is valuable less because it introduces a fundamentally new interview theory and more because it turns customer discovery into a **small, executable learning loop**. Relative to *The Mom Test*, Portigal, and Alvarez, Constable is particularly useful for the operational layer: deciding what you need to learn, getting in front of appropriate humans quickly, conducting lightweight interviews, documenting evidence, and immediately deciding what to investigate next.

I would therefore weight its distinctive operational practices more heavily and compress its overlap with the stronger interview-methodology books.

\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

## 1\. Start With the Learning Goal, Not the Interview Script

**Classification:** Principle

**Explanation**

Before deciding what questions to ask, explicitly determine **what uncertainty you are trying to reduce**.

An interview should not be:

“Let's talk to some construction companies and see what they say.”

It should be closer to:

“We need to understand how small construction companies currently coordinate last-minute worker reassignment between sites and whether failures create meaningful operational consequences.”

The learning objective constrains:

* whom to interview;
* what situations to explore;
* what questions matter;
* what observations count as useful evidence.

This prevents interviews from degenerating into interesting but commercially irrelevant conversations.

**Example**

Weak objective:

Learn whether contractors would like better scheduling software.

Better objective:

Learn how owners currently notice and resolve worker/site scheduling conflicts, how frequently conflicts occur, who gets involved, and what happens when they are not resolved quickly.

**AI interviewer implementation**

Maintain an explicit session-level object:

```
learning_goal:
  uncertainty: "How painful are worker reassignment problems?"
  evidence_needed:
    - recent real incidents
    - frequency
    - operational consequences
    - current workaround
    - people involved
  irrelevant_topics:
    - preferred UI
    - hypothetical features

```

Every question generated by the AI should be traceable to one of those evidence needs.

If conversation drifts, the interviewer should redirect toward the learning objective.

**Materially different?**

**Moderately.**

The Mom Test focuses more heavily on question quality and evidence reliability. Torres focuses on story extraction. Alvarez uses customer/problem hypotheses.

Constable's useful contribution is the very lightweight discipline:

**decide what you need to learn before talking to people.**

This should be an explicit harness primitive.

# 2\. Turn Assumptions Into Questions to Investigate

**Classification:** Principle / Evidence rule

**Explanation**

Discovery starts from assumptions, whether the founder admits it or not. Make them explicit before interviewing.

Instead of unconsciously trying to validate:

“Small contractors need scheduling software.”

break it into things that can actually be investigated:

* managers frequently need to reshuffle workers;
* this is currently handled manually;
* errors occur;
* errors have meaningful consequences;
* existing tools do not solve the problem adequately.

Interviews then investigate those assumptions rather than validating the overall idea.

**Example**

Assumption:

“Missing worker reports create invoicing problems.”

Questions should investigate:

“Tell me about the last time information from a Baustelle was missing when you needed to prepare an invoice.”

followed by:

“What happened?”

“What did you do?”

“Who else had to get involved?”

**AI interviewer implementation**

Before interviewing, decompose the founder's thesis:

```
Idea:
"Automate construction project reporting."

Assumptions:
A1: reporting is currently manual
A2: manual reporting creates delays
A3: delays have significant consequences
A4: companies already attempt workarounds
A5: someone has authority/budget to improve it

```

The interviewer should seek evidence for and against each assumption.

Do not ask respondents to validate A1–A5 directly.

**Materially different?**

**Low to moderate.**

This overlaps substantially with Lean Customer Development and Testing Business Ideas.

Constable's contribution is making this process accessible enough that a founder can use it immediately without a large discovery framework.

# 3\. Choose People Based on What They Can Teach You

**Classification:** Recruiting technique

**Explanation**

Recruiting is part of research design.

Do not simply interview whoever is easiest to reach.

Select participants who:

* actually encounter the situation being investigated;
* participate in the relevant behavior;
* represent the hypothesized customer or user;
* can speak from direct experience.

Different uncertainties may require different interviewees.

An owner may understand purchasing and business consequences.

A site manager may understand coordination.

A field worker may understand how instructions actually arrive.

**Example**

Research question:

Why are daily site reports incomplete?

Interviewing only company owners risks producing second-hand explanations.

Useful participants might include:

* workers creating the reports;
* Bauleiter consuming them;
* office employees processing them;
* owners affected when reporting failures become financial problems.

**AI interviewer implementation**

Before recruiting, produce:

```
Learning question:
Why are site reports incomplete?

Knowledge holders:
- field worker → creation behavior
- Bauleiter → enforcement/workflow
- office admin → downstream consequences
- owner → business impact

Priority:
1. field workers
2. Bauleiter
3. office
4. owner

```

Also record the interviewee's relationship to the workflow so second-hand claims can be discounted.

**Materially different?**

**Moderate.**

Portigal covers participant selection much more rigorously.

Constable is useful because he ties participant choice directly to **what needs to be learned**, which works well in a lightweight founder workflow.

# 4\. Recruit Creatively Instead of Treating Recruiting as a Research Project

**Classification:** Recruiting technique

**Explanation**

A founder should not wait for a perfect research panel.

Go to places where relevant people already exist.

Possible channels include:

* personal introductions;
* customers or prospects;
* professional communities;
* industry events;
* online groups;
* physical locations where users congregate;
* people one or two connections away.

The objective is to create enough access to start learning.

The important constraint is relevance—not methodological perfection.

**Example**

Instead of spending weeks building a list of construction-company executives, a founder investigating field coordination might get initial conversations through:

* an existing contractor client;
* the client's subcontractors;
* suppliers;
* Bauleiter contacts;
* trade associations;
* local industry groups.

**AI interviewer implementation**

Given the target persona and learning goal, generate a recruiting map:

```
Target:
Owners of 10–50 employee construction firms

Possible access channels:
- existing customer referrals
- subcontractor network
- local construction associations
- supplier relationships
- LinkedIn
- industry WhatsApp/Facebook groups

Recruiting objective:
5–10 conversations from people directly exposed to the workflow

```

The system should distinguish:

**easy-to-reach** from **actually relevant**.

**Materially different?**

**Moderate.**

Alvarez covers recruiting more deeply. Portigal covers formal research recruiting.

Constable's distinctive value is founder-oriented pragmatism: **find humans and start learning rather than overengineering recruitment.**

# 5\. Ask for Help or Learning, Not for a Sales Meeting

**Classification:** Recruiting technique

**Explanation**

Discovery interviews are easier to recruit when the request is genuinely framed around learning rather than disguising a sales pitch.

Participants should understand that the founder wants to understand their world rather than pressure them into buying something.

This also changes respondent behavior: people tend to become more candid when they are not defending themselves against a salesperson.

**Example**

Instead of:

“Can I show you our new construction management platform?”

use something like:

“I'm trying to understand how smaller construction companies coordinate workers across multiple sites. Could I ask you about how you currently handle it?”

**AI interviewer implementation**

Generate outreach language that contains:

```
- why this person is relevant
- what the founder is trying to understand
- short time commitment
- no product pitch

```

If the founder already has a solution, the AI should prevent the outreach from becoming disguised demo recruitment.

**Materially different?**

**Moderate.**

The anti-pitch principle overlaps strongly with *The Mom Test*.

Constable contributes practical recruiting application of that principle.

# 6\. Prepare a Small Number of Critical Questions

**Classification:** Interview technique

**Explanation**

Do not write a huge questionnaire and mechanically run through it.

Prepare a small set of important areas tied to the learning objective, then let the conversation produce follow-up questions.

This gives the interviewer both:

* enough structure to collect comparable evidence;
* enough flexibility to investigate unexpected discoveries.

**Example**

Learning objective:

Understand job-site schedule disruption.

Prepared anchors:

1. Tell me about the last time the worker plan changed unexpectedly.
2. What caused the change?
3. How did everyone find out?
4. What happened because of it?
5. What did you do afterward?

The actual interview may spend 15 minutes on question #1 if the incident contains important evidence.

**AI interviewer implementation**

Use two layers:

```
Anchor questions
↓
dynamic probes

```

The AI should never treat the anchors as a mandatory questionnaire.

It should track coverage separately:

```
coverage:
  trigger: ✓
  workflow: ✓
  consequence: ✓
  workaround: ?
  frequency: ?

```

and naturally probe missing evidence.

**Materially different?**

**Low.**

Portigal and Hansen provide considerably deeper interviewing technique.

Constable adds a useful lightweight implementation rule.

# 7\. Ask About Reality, Not Predictions

**Classification:** Evidence rule / Interview technique

**Explanation**

Evidence about actual behavior is generally more useful than speculation about future behavior.

Prefer questions about:

* what happened;
* what someone did;
* what they currently use;
* what they spent;
* how they solved the problem;
* what the consequence was.

Avoid relying on:

* “Would you use...?”
* “Would this be useful?”
* “Would you pay for...?”

**Example**

Weak:

“Would you use an app that automatically schedules your workers?”

Better:

“How did you decide yesterday which workers went to which construction site?”

Then:

“What happens when someone calls in sick?”

**AI interviewer implementation**

Add a question-quality classifier.

```
"What would you do if...?"
→ hypothetical
→ rewrite

"Tell me about the last time..."
→ behavioral evidence
→ preferred

```

When the respondent voluntarily makes a prediction:

“I would definitely use something like that.”

the AI should follow with:

“How are you dealing with that problem today?”

**Materially different?**

**Very low.**

This is central to *The Mom Test* and well covered by Torres and Alvarez.

Do not give Constable significant independent weighting here.

# 8\. Look for Specific Stories Rather Than General Opinions

**Classification:** Interview technique / Evidence rule

**Explanation**

General statements hide the details needed to understand behavior.

If someone says:

“Communication is always a problem.”

the interviewer should get to an actual incident.

Specific stories reveal:

* sequence;
* context;
* people;
* tools;
* triggers;
* obstacles;
* consequences;
* workarounds.

**Example**

Respondent:

“The workers don't send reports properly.”

Probe:

“Can you walk me through the last time that happened?”

Then reconstruct:

```
Friday afternoon
↓
worker leaves site
↓
photos missing
↓
Bauleiter notices Monday
↓
office calls worker
↓
worker searches phone
↓
invoice preparation delayed

```

That sequence is far more useful than the original complaint.

**AI interviewer implementation**

Detect abstract language such as:

* usually;
* often;
* normally;
* people tend to;
* we generally;
* it can be difficult.

Trigger:

specific-event probe.

Store the resulting episode as a separate evidence object.

**Materially different?**

**Very low.**

Torres and Portigal provide substantially stronger treatments of story elicitation.

\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

# 9\. Avoid Leading the Witness

**Classification:** Anti-pattern

**Explanation**

Questions should not contain the answer the founder hopes to hear.

Bad discovery frequently looks like:

“Wouldn't it save you a lot of time if this were automated?”

The interviewee is now responding to the founder's proposition rather than describing reality.

Leading questions create false confidence.

**Example**

Leading:

“Is scheduling workers manually frustrating?”

Neutral:

“How do you currently schedule workers?”

Then:

“What parts of that process are difficult?”

**AI interviewer implementation**

Before emitting a question, detect:

```
- embedded assumptions
- positive framing
- solution language
- desired answers
- emotionally loaded words

```

Transform:

“How annoying is manually entering the hours?”

into:

“What happens when working hours are submitted?”

Only introduce “annoying” if the respondent has already characterized it that way.

**Materially different?**

**Very low.**

This is foundational throughout interview literature, especially *The Mom Test*.

\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

# 10\. Observe What People Do, Not Only What They Say

**Classification:** Evidence rule

**Explanation**

Constable encourages founders to notice observations beyond verbal answers.

Useful discovery can come from:

* seeing the tools someone uses;
* watching the sequence of work;
* noticing physical artifacts;
* observing improvised workarounds;
* seeing where people switch systems;
* identifying discrepancies between claimed and actual behavior.

This is particularly useful when interviewing in the customer's environment.

**Example**

A manager explains:

“We manage everything digitally.”

But during the conversation you observe:

* a whiteboard with tomorrow's worker assignments;
* WhatsApp running on the manager's phone;
* printed project folders;
* handwritten protocol numbers.

Those artifacts may reveal more about the actual system than the statement “we manage everything digitally.”

**AI interviewer implementation**

Allow interviewer notes to distinguish:

```
CLAIM:
"We use software for scheduling."

OBSERVATION:
Manager checked handwritten weekly roster during explanation.

INTERPRETATION:
Potential parallel/manual scheduling process.

```

Never collapse observations and interpretations into the same data field.

**Materially different?**

**Moderate to high for the harness.**

Portigal goes much deeper into contextual research, but this is an important reminder that a founder's evidence model should support **observations as well as interview answers**.

This is worth retaining.

# 11\. Separate Facts, Quotes, and Interpretation

**Classification:** Evidence rule

**Explanation**

Record what the participant actually said or did before converting it into conclusions.

Otherwise the founder begins writing:

“Customers need automated scheduling.”

when the evidence was actually:

“Yesterday I spent an hour calling six workers after someone became sick.”

Those are not equivalent.

The first is an interpretation or solution hypothesis.

The second is evidence.

**Example**

Store:

```
Evidence:
Owner called six workers after a sick leave notification.

Quote:
"I basically spent the whole morning rearranging everyone."

Interpretation:
Worker reassignment may create meaningful coordination overhead.

Potential opportunity:
Reduce reassignment coordination effort.

```

**AI interviewer implementation**

Use distinct fields:

```
observation
verbatim_or_paraphrased_statement
inference
opportunity
solution_hypothesis

```

Never allow the model to silently turn the first two into the latter three.

**Materially different?**

**Moderate.**

The underlying research principle is not unique, but it is particularly important for an AI harness because language models naturally compress evidence into conclusions.

This should therefore receive **more implementation weight than its originality would suggest**.

# 12\. Capture Notes Immediately

**Classification:** Evidence rule

**Explanation**

Memory decays extremely quickly after interviews.

Important details include:

* exact terminology;
* emotional reactions;
* contradictions;
* unexpected observations;
* workflow details;
* follow-up questions.

Capture these during the session where possible and consolidate them immediately afterward.

**Example**

Immediately after an interview:

```
Unexpected:
Bauleiter does not control worker schedule; office manager does.

Strong pain:
Missing daily reports delay invoicing.

Existing workaround:
Office admin phones workers every Monday.

Follow-up:
Ask next interview how widespread Monday chasing is.

```

**AI interviewer implementation**

Automatically generate a post-interview evidence packet:

```
1. strongest evidence
2. contradictory evidence
3. observed behavior
4. unresolved questions
5. assumptions affected
6. new hypotheses
7. suggested next interview target

```

The raw transcript should remain available underneath.

**Materially different?**

**Moderate.**

Portigal covers research documentation more thoroughly.

Constable makes the **immediate debrief loop** particularly compatible with fast founder discovery.

\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

# 13\. Debrief Every Interview Before Conducting Many More

**Classification:** Synthesis rule

**Explanation**

Customer discovery should be iterative rather than:

```
20 interviews
↓
massive synthesis exercise

```

Instead:

```
Interview
↓
Debrief
↓
Adjust hypothesis/questions
↓
Interview
↓
Debrief

```

The interview process itself should evolve as new evidence appears.

**Example**

Interview #1 reveals that worker availability is not the real scheduling problem; subcontractor dependencies are.

Interview #2 should therefore include questions about subcontractors rather than blindly running the original questionnaire.

**AI interviewer implementation**

After each interview, update:

```
confirmed assumptions
weakened assumptions
new assumptions
uncertainties
question changes
participant-target changes

```

Generate the next interview plan from the updated evidence state.

**Materially different?**

**Moderate to high.**

Continuous Discovery Habits later develops continuous discovery much more systematically.

But Constable gives a very simple founder-friendly version of the loop.

# 14\. Look for Repeated Evidence, Not Individual Quotes

**Classification:** Synthesis rule

**Explanation**

One memorable customer story is not automatically a market.

Patterns matter.

Look across interviews for repetition in:

* situations;
* behaviors;
* problems;
* workarounds;
* consequences;
* language;
* actors.

At the same time, do not reduce synthesis to merely counting mentions. A rare but extremely expensive problem may matter more than a frequently mentioned annoyance.

**Example**

Across six companies:

```
5 use WhatsApp for urgent worker coordination
4 manually reconstruct working hours
3 chase workers for missing reports
2 experienced invoice delays because evidence was missing

```

The evidence starts suggesting workflows worth deeper investigation.

**AI interviewer implementation**

Cluster evidence by:

```
job
trigger
problem
workflow
workaround
consequence
persona
context

```

Then display:

```
frequency
number of independent companies
severity indicators
contradictory cases

```

Never output:

“Customers clearly need X”

merely because several related statements occurred.

**Materially different?**

**Low to moderate.**

Alvarez and Torres provide stronger synthesis methodologies.

Constable contributes the lightweight iterative habit rather than sophisticated synthesis mechanics.

\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

# 15\. Treat Surprises and Contradictions as Valuable Evidence

**Classification:** Evidence rule

**Explanation**

Discovery is valuable when reality contradicts the founder's model.

An interview that disproves an assumption is successful research.

The goal is not accumulating confirming statements.

**Example**

Founder assumes:

Bauleiter allocate workers.

Interviews reveal:

In many companies the office manager or owner actually makes allocation decisions.

That changes:

* target user;
* workflow model;
* product design;
* sales conversation.

**AI interviewer implementation**

Explicitly reward contradiction detection.

After every interview ask:

```
What did we learn that contradicts the current model?

```

Maintain:

```
assumption_confidence:
  supportive_evidence
  contradictory_evidence

```

The system should actively surface contradictions instead of averaging them away.

**Materially different?**

**Low.**

Lean Customer Development and experimentation literature cover falsification and confirmation bias more explicitly.

Still important as a harness rule.

\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

# 16\. Convert Each Interview Into a Decision About What to Learn Next

**Classification:** Synthesis rule

**Explanation**

Research has little value unless it changes behavior.

A useful interview should potentially change:

* the customer hypothesis;
* the problem hypothesis;
* participant targeting;
* interview questions;
* the product concept;
* what experiment to run next;
* whether an idea deserves further attention.

Discovery is therefore a sequence of decisions, not an archive of transcripts.

**Example**

Evidence:

Companies rarely lose money because schedules change, but missing site documentation repeatedly delays invoicing.

Decision:

Deprioritize scheduling investigation and conduct five interviews specifically around documentation-to-invoice workflows.

**AI interviewer implementation**

Every synthesis should end with:

```
Decision:
Continue / investigate / deprioritize / test

Reason:
Evidence supporting the decision

Remaining uncertainty:
What still needs to be known

Next learning action:
Who to interview or what to observe next

```

This is much stronger than generating only a summary.

**Materially different?**

**High practical value, moderate originality.**

Testing Business Ideas provides a far richer experiment framework, while Torres provides a stronger discovery framework.

Constable's version is useful because it creates a simple **research → decision → next research** loop suitable for a solo founder.

# 17\. Do Not Turn Discovery Into Pitching

**Classification:** Anti-pattern

**Explanation**

Founders naturally want to explain their idea.

Once they start doing so, the interview changes.

The respondent begins reacting to the proposed product instead of revealing their existing reality.

Pitching also produces compliments and politeness that can easily be misread as evidence.

**Example**

Bad:

“We're developing an AI system that automatically generates site reports. Would that solve this?”

Better:

“What happens after a worker finishes work for the day?”

and later:

“What happens if the report isn't submitted?”

**AI interviewer implementation**

Maintain a **discovery mode** in which solution descriptions are suppressed.

If the interviewee asks:

“So what exactly are you building?”

the AI can provide a deliberately minimal response and return the conversation to the respondent.

**Materially different?**

**Very low.**

*The Mom Test* handles this much more strongly.

Treat Constable as corroboration rather than a separate methodology here.

\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

# 18\. Do Not Outsource Discovery Completely

**Classification:** Anti-pattern / Principle

**Explanation**

Early customer conversations teach founders things that are difficult to capture through reports alone:

* vocabulary;
* domain structure;
* customer psychology;
* unexpected constraints;
* emotional reactions;
* subtle workflow details.

The founder should therefore remain directly exposed to customers, especially while the business model and problem understanding are immature.

For an AI-assisted interviewing system, this becomes particularly important.

AI should amplify founder learning rather than becoming a wall between founder and customer.

**Example**

Bad operating model:

```
AI conducts 100 interviews
→ founder receives "top 10 customer pain points"

```

Better:

```
AI assists interview
→ preserves transcript/evidence
→ detects patterns
→ founder reviews important stories
→ founder participates directly in key conversations

```

**AI interviewer implementation**

Require traceability.

For every synthesized opportunity allow the founder to inspect:

```
opportunity
↓
supporting interviews
↓
specific stories
↓
specific evidence

```

Surface particularly surprising interviews for human review.

**Materially different?**

**Potentially high for this project.**

The book predates modern AI interviewing, obviously, but its emphasis on direct founder contact has an important implication:

**Do not optimize the harness so aggressively that the founder stops learning from customers personally.**

# Common Mistakes Worth Encoding

**Classification:** Anti-patterns

Most overlap with the other books, so these need only become guardrails:

| Mistake                                                 | Harness response                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Interviewing friends because they are convenient        | Check participant relevance             |
| Asking whether someone likes the idea                   | Redirect to current/past behavior       |
| Asking future hypotheticals                             | Ask for a recent real example           |
| Pitching during discovery                               | Suppress solution explanation           |
| Asking leading questions                                | Rewrite neutrally                       |
| Talking too much                                        | Monitor interviewer/respondent ratio    |
| Collecting interesting facts unrelated to learning goal | Mark as peripheral                      |
| Treating one enthusiastic respondent as validation      | Require cross-interview evidence        |
| Ignoring contradictions                                 | Surface them explicitly                 |
| Taking weak notes                                       | Generate immediate debrief              |
| Conducting many interviews without adapting             | Update learning plan after each session |
| Producing summaries with no action                      | Require next learning decision          |

# Recommended Weight in Your Harness

I would not create a large independent **“Constable methodology layer.”** Most of the interview mechanics are already better represented by Fitzpatrick, Portigal, Hansen, Torres, and Alvarez.

What *Talking to Humans* should contribute is primarily a lightweight **discovery execution loop**:

```
ASSUMPTION
    ↓
WHAT DO WE NEED TO LEARN?
    ↓
WHO CAN TEACH US?
    ↓
GET ACCESS TO THEM
    ↓
ASK ABOUT REAL BEHAVIOR
    ↓
CAPTURE STORIES + OBSERVATIONS
    ↓
DEBRIEF IMMEDIATELY
    ↓
WHAT CHANGED?
    ↓
WHAT DO WE NEED TO LEARN NEXT?
    ↓
NEXT INTERVIEW

```

That is the part worth preserving.

# Unique Contributions of Talking to Humans

The strongest distinctive contributions for your harness are:

1. **Learning-goal-first interview design** Explicitly determine the uncertainty to reduce before generating questions.
2. **Founder-friendly assumption → interview loop** Translate implicit business assumptions into things that can be investigated immediately without requiring a heavy research framework.
3. **Pragmatic recruiting** Identify who has the relevant knowledge and find practical ways of reaching them instead of waiting for perfect research recruitment.
4. **Observation as evidence** Capture what people actually do, use, show, and improvise—not merely what they tell the interviewer.
5. **Immediate post-interview debriefing** Every conversation should update assumptions, questions, participant targeting, and the next learning action.
6. **Research must produce a next decision** Interviews are not complete when they are summarized. They are complete when they change what the founder investigates or does next.
7. **Lightweight iterative discovery cadence** Interview → learn → adjust → interview is preferable to designing a giant fixed research study upfront.
8. **Founder exposure matters** Particularly relevant to an AI harness: AI should structure, capture, probe, and synthesize customer conversations, but should not turn the founder into a passive recipient of generated summaries.

The final point is arguably the most important design implication for your system. A fully autonomous interviewer that gives you beautifully synthesized “customer insights” could actually make you **worse at customer discovery** if it removes the direct exposure through which founders build domain intuition. The harness should automate cognitive overhead while preserving access to the underlying stories and evidence.
